\propGLPDeduction*

\begin{proof}
The $/\!?$ operator replaces every reader $X?$ by its paired writer $X$, transforming GLP terms into LP terms. We show that the cGLP run $\rho$ corresponds to an LP run $\rho/?$ of $LP(P/?)$.

Consider a cGLP transition $(G, \sigma) \rightarrow (G', \sigma')$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Reduce transition}: Goal $A$ reduces with clause $C$ via writer mgu $\hat\sigma$, and $(\hat\sigma?)^\star$ is applied to the body. Applying $/\!?$, the clause $C/?$ is an LP clause, and $A/?$ unifies with the head $H/?$ via the mgu $\hat\sigma/? = (\hat\sigma?)^\star/?$ (since writers map to writers), which LP applies to the body. This is a valid LP reduction.
    \item \emph{Communicate transition}: A reader $X? \in G$ is replaced by the value $T$ assigned to its paired writer. Under $/\!?$, both $X?$ and $X$ map to $X$, so this transition becomes the identity---the variable $X$ is already assigned $T$ in the LP view.
\end{itemize}

Thus each cGLP transition corresponds to zero or one LP transitions, and the cGLP run $\rho$ projects to an LP run $\rho/?$ of $LP(P/?)$. By standard LP soundness, the outcome of $\rho/?$ is a logical consequence of $P/?$.
\end{proof}

\propSOPreservation*

\begin{proof}
By induction on the length of the run. The base case is immediate: $G_0$ satisfies SO by assumption.

For the inductive step, assume $G$ satisfies SO and consider a transition $(G, \sigma) \rightarrow (G', \sigma')$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Reduce transition}: Goal $A \in G$ reduces with clause $C = (H \mathrel{\mbox{\texttt{:-}}} B)$ via writer mgu $\hat\sigma$, yielding $G' = (G \setminus \{A\}) \cup B(\hat\sigma?)^\star$. Since $C$ satisfies SRSW, it satisfies SO. Since $C$ is renamed apart from $G$, the variables in $B$ are fresh. $\hat\sigma$ binds the writers of $H$ to pairwise disjoint subterms of $A$ and the writers of $A$ to pairwise disjoint subterms of $H$, a writer of $H$ occurs nowhere in $B$ and its reader at most once in $C$ (by SO of $C$), and a reader of a writer of $A$ occurs at most once in $G$ (by SO of $G$), so $B(\hat\sigma?)^\star$ satisfies SO, and no variable of $A$ occurs in $G \setminus \{A\}$ (by SO of $G$). Since $G \setminus \{A\}$ satisfies SO, $B$ satisfies SO, and no variable of $B$ occurs in $G$ (by renaming apart), $G'$ satisfies SO.

    \item \emph{Communicate transition}: $G' = G\{X? := T\}$ with $\{X? := T\} \in \sigma$. Since $G$ satisfies SO, $X?$ occurs at most once in $G$. Replacing that occurrence by $T$, which satisfies SO (Definition~\ref{def:writers-assignment}), preserves SO provided no variable of $T$ occurs elsewhere in $G$. A variable occurs in at most one term $\sigma$ assigns: a clause variable occurs once in its clause, and matching binds the writers of a head to pairwise disjoint subterms of the goal. A variable of an assigned term enters a goal only by the Communicate of that assignment, since a Reduce brings into the goal the variables of a clause renamed apart, which by the proper run condition (Definition~\ref{def:cglp-proper-run}) are new to the configuration, and variables of the reduced goal, which it removes. So no variable of $T$ occurs in $G$, and $G'$ satisfies SO.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\propMonotonicity*

\begin{proof}
Suppose goal $A$ can reduce with clause $C$ at step $i$, meaning the writer mgu of $A$ and the head $H$ of (a renaming of) $C$ succeeds. Consider what can change between steps $i$ and $j > i$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Reduce transitions on other goals}: These do not affect $A$ directly. By SO, no other goal shares a writer with $A$, so no other reduction can assign a writer in $A$.

    \item \emph{Communicate transitions}: These instantiate readers, not writers. A Communicate transition $X? := T$ may instantiate a reader $X? \in A$, yielding $A' = A\{X? := T\}$. We show $A'$ can still reduce with $C$:

    The original writer mgu succeeded, meaning at position $p$ where $X?$ occurred in $A$, $H$ had a writer $Y$ at position $p$, yielding assignment $Y := X?$; a reader or a non-variable term of $H$ at position $p$ would have caused failure or suspension, respectively, contradicting the success of the writer mgu.

    After the Communicate transition, $A'$ has $T$ at position $p$. The clause $C$ (renamed apart from $A'$) has a fresh writer $Y'$ at position $p$. The writer mgu now yields $Y' := T$, which succeeds.

    \item \emph{Reduce transition on $A$}: If $A$ itself is reduced at some step $k$ with $i < k \le j$, then an instance of $A$ has been reduced, satisfying the proposition.
\end{itemize}

Thus, if $A$ has not been reduced by step $j$, the (possibly instantiated) goal $A'$ at step $j$ can still reduce with a fresh renaming of $C$.
\end{proof}

\corSOGuards*

\begin{proof}
Let a Reduce transition reduce $A$ with the renamed guarded clause $H$ \texttt{:-} $G$ \texttt{|} $B$ via writer mgu $\hat\sigma$. For each ground-guarded writer $X$, the success of $G\hat\sigma?$ implies that $X?\hat\sigma?$ is ground (Definition~\ref{def:ground-guarded}). So $\bar\theta = \theta \cup \theta?$, with $\theta$ the restriction of $(\hat\sigma?)^\star$ to the ground-guarded writers, is a ground instantiation of the clause, and $(H$ \texttt{:-} $B)\bar\theta$ satisfies SO (Definition~\ref{def:guarded-srsw}). $A$ and $H\bar\theta$ have the writer mgu $\hat\sigma'$ obtained from $\hat\sigma$ by removing the bindings of the ground-guarded writers and applying $\bar\theta$ to its terms, and $B(\hat\sigma?)^\star = (B\bar\theta)(\hat\sigma'?)^\star$. So the transition is the Reduce of $A$ with the clause $(H$ \texttt{:-} $B)\bar\theta$, which satisfies SO, and the guard adds no variable to the configuration; the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:so-preservation} applies.
\end{proof}

\propMaGLPSOPreservation*

\begin{proof}
Reduce $p$ preserves SO at agent $p$ by the same argument as cGLP SO Preservation (Proposition~\ref{prop:so-preservation}), restricted to agent $p$'s resolvent. Communicate $p$ to $q$ removes $\{X? := T\}$ from $\sigma_p$ (no change to $G_p$) and applies $\{X? := T\}$ to $G_q$. The substitution preserves SO in $G_q$ provided no variable of $T$ occurs in $G_q$; this holds as in the cGLP case: a variable occurs in at most one assigned term, and enters a goal only by the Communicate of that assignment. For Cold-call $p$ to $q$, the message \verb|msg|$(q,X)$ is transferred from $p$'s network output stream to $q$'s network input stream. The writers in $X$ are no longer reachable from $p$'s resolvent after advancing (the resolvent has iterated past the consumed stream element), so they occur only in $q$'s resolvent. Input $p$ adds $\{X? := T\}$ to $\sigma_p$ and changes no goal; the variables of $T$ are fresh, so they occur in no goal until the Communicate of that assignment, which is the cGLP case. SO is preserved.
\end{proof}

\propMaGLPDeduction*

\begin{proof}
Let $r = c^0 \rightarrow c^1 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow c^n$ be a finite proper maGLP run over $P$ and $M$, with $c^k_p = (G^k_p, \sigma^k_p)$ for $p \in P$. We construct a proper cGLP run $\hat r$ of $L_r$ such that, after simulating the $k$-th maGLP step, the goal of $\hat r$ contains $\bigcup_{p \in P} G^k_p$ and its substitution contains $\bigcup_{p \in P} \sigma^k_p$.

The initial goal of $L_r$ is $\bigcup_{p \in P} G^0_p$ together with the \verb|network| goal and the \verb|person|$_p$ goals. It satisfies SO: within each agent's initial goal every variable occurs once, the agents' initial goals are variable-disjoint, the variables of each agent's being its own (Definition~\ref{def:agent-variables}), and each agent's network-channel variables are paired one-for-one with those of the \verb|network| goal, and its person-channel variables with those of its \verb|person|$_p$ goal. Hence by SO Preservation (Proposition~\ref{prop:so-preservation}) every goal of $\hat r$ satisfies SO. We simulate each maGLP transaction.

\emph{Reduce $p$.} The step is a cGLP Reduce of some $A \in G^k_p$ with a clause $C \in M \subseteq M'_r$. Since $G^k_p$ is part of the goal of $\hat r$ and $C \in M'_r$, the identical reduction is a cGLP Reduce step of $L_r$, updating the goal and the substitution exactly as in maGLP and leaving the other agents' goals and the \verb|network| and \verb|person| goals untouched.

\emph{Input $p$.} The step adds $\{X? := T\}$ to $\sigma^k_p$, $X$ the writer of the tail of $p$'s person input stream or a writer in a message $p$ put on its person output stream. In $L_r$ the \verb|person|$_p$ goal holds $X$: the input stream's tail from the initial goal, and a message's writer once a Communicate delivers the message to it, which $\hat r$ takes first where $r$ has not. The clause of \verb|person|$_p$ for this input has $T$ at the position of $X$, so the Reduce of the \verb|person|$_p$ goal with it has the writer mgu $\{X := T\}$, adding $\{X? := T\}$ to the substitution of $\hat r$, as in maGLP; $r$ is finite, so \verb|person|$_p$ has a clause per input.

\emph{Communicate $p$ to $q$.} Here $\{X? := T\} \in \sigma^k_p$ and $X?$ occurs in $G^k_q$. Both lie in the substitution and the goal of $\hat r$, so the cGLP Communicate applying $\{X? := T\}$ is a step of $L_r$; as $X?$ occurs once in the goal, it instantiates exactly the occurrence in $q$'s goal, as in maGLP. The maGLP step removes $\{X? := T\}$ from $\sigma^k_p$ whereas the cGLP rule retains it, but $X?$ then occurs nowhere, so neither any subsequent goal nor the outcome is affected. When $p = q$ the transaction is unary and is itself a cGLP Communicate step of $L_r$.

\emph{Cold-call $p$ to $q$.} The network output stream of $c^k_p$ has a new message \verb|msg|$(q, X)$, and the step adds \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ to the network input stream of $c^k_q$. In $L_r$ the \verb|network| goal consumes \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ from $p$'s network output stream and places \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ on $q$'s network input stream, routing by the network-switch technique, generalised to $P$. This is a bounded sequence of cGLP steps of $L_r$ --- a Communicate delivering \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ to the \verb|network| goal, the Reduce of the routing clause, and a Communicate placing \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ on $q$'s network input stream --- after which \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ occurs on that stream, as in maGLP.

Each maGLP step is simulated by cGLP transitions of $L_r$, so $\hat r$ is a run of $L_r$. Reduce introduces only variables renamed apart, Input only fresh variables, and Communicate none, so $\hat r$ inherits properness from $r$. By cGLP Computation is Deduction (Proposition~\ref{prop:cglp-deduction}) applied to $L_r$ over $M'_r$, the outcome of $\hat r$ is a logical consequence of $M'_r/?$.
\end{proof}

\propObliviousInput*

\begin{proof}
The proof of the Obliviousness Condition (Proposition~\ref{proposition:oblivious}) in~\cite{lewis2026volitional} applies, with one addition to each of its two parts. Let $e$ be an interleaving of a correct run $r$ of $\calF^I(P)$ and a correct run of $\calF^I(P')$. \emph{Safety}: a $P$-step of $e$ by a transition of $I(P){\uparrow}P$ is a transition of $I(P \cup P'){\uparrow}(P \cup P')$, since $I(P) \subseteq I(P \cup P')$ and the agents of $P'$ are stationary in it; every other step is as there. \emph{Liveness}: the argument there concerns the classes of $\calF^I(P \cup P')$, which are those of $R(P \cup P'){\uparrow}(P \cup P')$, a transition of $I$ being in none; and the runs $r$ and $r'$ it draws on are correct runs of $\calF^I(P)$ and $\calF^I(P')$, whose transitions of $I$ owe nothing to liveness.
\end{proof}

\thmInteractiveGrassroots*

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{proposition:oblivious-input}, the protocol is oblivious. If it is also interactive, it is grassroots by Definition~\ref{definition:grassroots}.
\end{proof}

\thmMaGLPGrassroots*

\begin{proof}
Let $M$ name no agent and use cold calls, and $\calF$ be the maGLP protocol over $M$. By Theorem~\ref{theorem:interactive-grassroots}, it suffices to show that $\calF$ is a transactions-based protocol with input over a contained set of transactions, that it satisfies the condition of Proposition~\ref{proposition:oblivious-input}, and that it is interactive.

\emph{Transactions-based with input}: Let $R$ be the Reduce, Communicate and Cold-call transactions of Definition~\ref{definition:maGLP} over every finite set of agents, $I$ its Input transactions, and $\sim$ the maGLP transaction equivalence. Every participant of every transaction changes its state, so distinct transactions have disjoint closures (Section~\ref{sec:mts}). A transaction among agents of $P$ between configurations of $S(P)^P$ is over $S(P)$, so $\calF(P)$, the maGLP transition system over $P$ and $M$ restricted to $S(P)^P$, is $(S(P)^P, \{s_0\}^P, R(P){\uparrow}P \cup I(P){\uparrow}P, {\sim}{\uparrow}P)$, and $\calF$ is the protocol over $R$, $S$, $\sim$ and input $I$.

\emph{Contained}: Let $t \in R$ have participants $Q \subseteq P$ and source in $S(P)^Q$. Every agent occurring in the target of $t$ occurs in its source or in $M$: Reduce $p$ replaces a goal by the body of a clause of $M$ renamed apart, instantiated by a writer mgu whose terms are subterms of the goal and the clause; Communicate $p$ to $q$ moves a term of $\sigma_p$ into $G_q$; Cold-call $p$ to $q$ moves \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ from $p$'s network output stream to $q$'s network input stream. As $M$ names no agent, the target is in $S(P)^Q$, and $t \in R(P)$.

\emph{Obliviousness condition}: A class whose transactions have participants spanning both $P$ and $P'$ is one of Communicate or Cold-call transactions between some $p \in P$ and $q \in P'$. In an interleaving of correct runs of $\calF(P)$ and $\calF(P')$, the state of $p$ is a state of the run of $\calF(P)$, in $S(P)$, so no message \verb|msg|$(q, X)$ is on $p$'s network output stream, and Cold-call $p$ to $q$ is not enabled. Communicate $p$ to $q$ requires $p$ and $q$ to share a variable pair. The variables at the agents of $P$ in a run of $\calF(P)$ are in $\bigcup_{p \in P}\hat\calV_p$: those of the initial state at $p$, those Reduce $p$ renames into and those Input $p$ writes are in $\hat\calV_p$ (Definition~\ref{definition:maGLP}), and Communicate and Cold-call move variables among the agents of $P$. Likewise for $P'$, and the two unions are disjoint (Definition~\ref{def:agent-variables}), so Communicate $p$ to $q$ is not enabled. The condition of Proposition~\ref{proposition:oblivious-input} is satisfied.

\emph{Interactivity}: Let $Q \subset \Pi$, $P, P' \subseteq Q$ be disjoint and nonempty, $\hat r$ a prefix of a correct run of $\calF(Q)$ containing no interaction between $P$ and $P'$, $p \in P$ and $q \in P'$. As $M$ uses cold calls, $\hat r$ extends, by an Input $p$ writing a term in which $q$ occurs and by Reduce $p$ and Communicate $p$ to $p$ transactions, to a finite safe run of $\calF(Q)$ at whose last configuration Cold-call $p$ to $q$ is enabled; extend it by that Cold-call. The Cold-call changes the states of $p$ and $q$, and $p$'s step is not a transition of $\calF(\{p\})$, its source naming $q \notin \{p\}$; so it is an interaction between $p$ and $q$ (Definition~\ref{definition:interaction}), and hence between $P$ and $P'$. The result is a finite safe run of $\calF(Q)$ having $\hat r$ as a prefix and containing an interaction between $P$ and $P'$, and by Lemma~\ref{lemma:extension} it is a prefix of a correct run of $\calF(Q)$. Hence $\calF$ is interactive.
\end{proof}

\corSocialGraphGrassroots*

\begin{proof}
The social graph program names no agent: its constants are message tags, and an agent's own identity and its friends' reach it through the person channel and the network. It uses cold calls: in every state, the person's \verb|connect(Target)| request naming an agent is followed by the reduction of the clause of Section~\ref{sec:ma-social-graph} that sends \verb|msg|$(\mathtt{Target}, \mathtt{intro}(\mathtt{Id}, \mathtt{Resp}))$ on the network output stream, at which Cold-call to \verb|Target| is enabled. By Theorem~\ref{theorem:maGLP-grassroots}, it is grassroots.
\end{proof}
