\section{Introduction}

A \emph{grassroots} platform~\cite{shapiro2023grassrootsBA,shapiro2025atomic} is a multiagent distributed system with multiple independent instances that can form and operate independently of each other and of any global resource other than the network, yet may coalesce into ever larger instances, possibly resulting in a single global instance.
Specifications of grassroots platforms that have been provided and proven grassroots include the grassroots social graph~\cite{shapiro2023gsn,shapiro2025atomic} --- the infrastructure platform on which all others build --- grassroots social networks~\cite{shapiro2023gsn,shapiro2026cssn}, grassroots cryptocurrencies and bonds~\cite{shapiro2024gc,lewis2023grassroots,shapiro2026bonds}, and grassroots federations~\cite{shapiro2025GF,halpern2024federated}; the unifying formal framework is volitional multiagent atomic transactions~\cite{lewis2026volitional}. Working GLP implementations of the social graph (this paper), child-safe social networks~\cite{shapiro2026cssn}, grassroots cryptocurrencies and bonds~\cite{shapiro2026bonds}, and secure recovery from major faults~\cite{eitan2026securing} have been derived by AI from these specifications. The Scuttlebutt protocol and social network~\cite{kermarrec2020gossiping} is perhaps the sole example of a deployed grassroots platform to date.
Grassroots platforms aim to offer an egalitarian/democratic alternative to centralised/autocratic and decentralised/plutocratic global platforms~\cite{shapiro2025characterising}.

Here, we present Grassroots Logic Programs (GLP), a multiagent, concurrent, logic programming language, designed for the implementation of smartphone-based grassroots platforms. Syntactically, GLP extends Logic Programs (LP)~\cite{lloyd1987foundations} with (1) \textbf{Readers:} Each logic variable (now referred to as \emph{writer}) is paired with a \emph{reader}, which is assigned a value only once its paired writer is assigned that value;  (2) \textbf{Single-Occurrence (SO):} A variable may occur at most once in a goal or a clause; and (3) \textbf{Single-Reader Single-Writer (SRSW):} A writer occurs in a clause iff its paired reader occurs in the clause.

The operational semantics of GLP is staged in two layers. First, we present \emph{Concurrent GLP} (cGLP), a standard interleaving-based concurrent (single-agent) operational semantics defined as a restriction of standard LP semantics, preserving computation-as-deduction~\cite{kowalski1974predicate} yet conjuring both linear logic~\cite{girard1987linear} and futures/promises~\cite{baker1977future,friedman1976impact}: An assignment to a variable may be produced at most once, via the sole occurrence of a writer (promise), and consumed at most once, via the sole occurrence of its paired reader (future). We illustrate cGLP via a grassroots social graph with a simulated network switch, in which agents may initiate friendships (bidirectional communication channels) via ``cold-calls'' as well as introduce mutual friends to each other.

Second, we present multiagent transition systems and their specification via multiagent atomic transactions, and use them to define the multiagent operational semantics of GLP, \emph{Multiagent GLP} (maGLP), with (1) \textbf{Multiagents:}  Named agents that operate independently while communicating to remote readers assignments made to paired local writers;  (2) \textbf{Cold-calls:} A means for sending a term with variables to a named agent while retaining their paired variables locally.
%
Cold-calls allow two agents in disconnected components of the social graph to become the owners of paired logic variables. We then present an maGLP implementation of the social graph in which the use of a network-simulator is replaced by maGLP's Cold-call transaction, and social cold-calls among agents are realised by maGLP cold-calls.
%
Lastly, we recall the definition of grassroots platforms and how to prove that a platform specified via atomic transactions is grassroots~\cite{shapiro2023grassrootsBA,shapiro2025atomic}, and apply these to prove that maGLP is indeed grassroots.

The mathematical foundations presented here are being used by AI to derive implementations of GLP, as well as to program in GLP, as detailed in companion papers: a workstation-based implementation of cGLP in Dart was derived from the operational semantics of cGLP; a smartphone-based implementation of maGLP in Dart/Flutter is being developed based on the operational semantics of maGLP~\cite{shapiro2026implementing}. A moded type system for GLP was designed (and implemented by AI in Dart) to facilitate collaborative human-AI development of GLP programs, where AI derives working GLP programs from human-agreed type definitions and declarations~\cite{shapiro2026types}. A secure implementation of GLP using public-key cryptography, mutual attestations, and friend-based identity custodians is being developed~\cite{eitan2026securing}.
Example GLP programs in this paper are typed.
