\begin{abstract}
A grassroots platform is a multiagent distributed systems that can have multiple independent instances that can form and operate independently of each other and of any global resource, yet may coalesce into ever larger instances, possibly resulting in a single global instance.  Grassroots platforms aim to offer an egalitarian/democratic alternative to  centralised/autocratic and decentralised/plutocratic global platforms.

Here, we present Grassroots Logic Programs (GLP), a multiagent concurrent logic programming language designed for the implementation of grassroots platforms.   To introduce the language, we recall the standard operational semantics of logic programs; introduce the operational semantics of Concurrent (single-agent) GLP as a restriction of that of LP; recall the notion of multiagent atomic transactions; use them to introduce the operational semantics of multiagent GLP; and prove multiagent GLP to be grassroots. The accompanying programming example is the grassroots social graph---the foundational grassroots platform on which all others are based. 

The mathematical foundations presented here are being used by AI to derive implementations of GLP, as well to program in GLP, as detailed in companion papers: a workstation-based implementation of Concurrent GLP in Dart was derived from the operational semantics of Concurrent GLP;  a smartphone-based implementation of multiagent GLP in Dart/Flutter is being developed based on the operational semantics of multiagent GLP. A moded type system for GLP was designed (and implemented by AI in Dart) to facilitate collaborative human-AI development of GLP programs, where AI derives working GLP programs from human-agreed type definitions and declarations. GLP implementations of grassroots platforms for the social graph, social networks, currencies and bonds, and more, are derived by AI from mathematical specifications via volitional multiagent transactions.

While concurrent logic programming and its powerful programming techniques have been known for four decades, adoption has been hampered by their inaccessibility to the average programmer; with AI as a hyper-programmer this limitation is removed.  Moreover, our experience suggests that the abstract nature of concurrent logic programming renders it a more powerful and productive language for collaborative human-AI specifications-based program development than mainstream languages.
\end{abstract}
